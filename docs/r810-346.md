# $R(8,10) \ge 346$

**A colouring of $K_{345}$ with no red $K_8$ and no blue $K_{10}$.** This improves the published
lower bound $R(8,10)\ge 345$ (Cremer, arXiv:2610.12122, 8 Oct 2026, a colouring of $K_{344}$), which
is the value currently carried by Table 2.1 of Radziszowski's survey as amended by that paper.

## The witness

`results/witnesses/r810-n345-k8-free-k10-free.txt`

* 345 × 345 matrix, rows of `0`/`1`, `1` = red; symmetric, zero diagonal.
* 26 045 red edges (and 33 195 blue).
* SHA-256 `0af4590108642651e3999994b379235f8b126bb9eb1fdfd36fb450ef47935b12`.

## Verification — four independent implementations

| checker | what it does | result |
|---|---|---|
| `count.c` | exact enumeration of red $K_8$ and blue $K_{10}$ | red $K_8 = 0$, blue $K_{10} = 0$ |
| `verify.c` | max-clique branch and bound on both colours | $\omega_{\text{red}} = 7$, $\omega_{\text{blue}} = 9$ |
| `verify2.c` | target-size DFS, no colouring bound, no shared code with `verify.c` | red $K_8$: none (10 471 027 nodes); blue $K_{10}$: none (195 066 549 nodes) → VALID |
| `two.c` | independent clique enumerator written for this session | conflicts = 0 → ALREADY VALID |

$\omega_{\text{red}} = 7 < 8$ and $\omega_{\text{blue}} = 9 < 10$, so no monochromatic clique of the
forbidden size exists; every colouring of $K_{345}$ is thus not forced to contain one, giving
$R(8,10)\ge 346$.

## How it was found

Cremer's method — add one vertex to the record colouring, then repair — driven far enough to reach
zero. From Cremer's $(8,10)$ colouring of $K_{344}$, the twin of vertex 335 joined by a blue edge
gives $K_{345}$ with 29 814 blue $K_{10}$ conflicts (this is the exact minimum over all $2\cdot344$
twin choices). Descending from there with exact-conflict repair, tabu, Metropolis and stall kicks,
restarting with fresh random seeds from the best state at every round:

$$29\,814 \to 97 \to 96 \to 54 \to 41 \to 26 \to 23 \to 11 \to 6 \to 2 \to 0$$

The final segment ran from the 6-conflict state and reached zero at iteration 159 966 after 2 770 s
(`.scratch/camp810/P222.log`). The witness differs from every intermediate state by hundreds of
edges — the last step from a 2-conflict state was a jump of 1 226 edge changes, not a small edit.

Engine and flags, for reproduction:

```
engine start.txt 8 10 0 run 20000000 32 <seed> 3600 1000 P<seed> 1.607 64
```

(`.ultramath/smvzvl5gk/m1/engine.c`; `start.txt` a 345-vertex colouring with `1` = red.)

## Two things this is not

* **Not a proof that $R(8,10)=346$.** The upper bound is far away (the survey's is in the
  thousands); this moves the lower end by one.
* **Not a product of the bounded-distance analysis.** That analysis had shown the $+1$ step to be
  $\ge 17$ edge changes from any colouring for $(5,6)$ and $(4,6)$, and no distance screen finishes
  at $n=345$ — so nothing ruled this in or out in advance. It was found by search, and the search
  had to cross a barrier that an exhaustive check proved was at least three coordinated flips wide
  from the 2-conflict state.

## Corrections to the record this produced

* During the campaign, `closer.c` reported `SOLVED depth=2` from a 2-conflict 345-vertex state and
  wrote back an unchanged matrix. An independent exhaustive check (all 73 candidate first flips, with
  the intersection condition for the second) proved that no 1- or 2-flip repair exists from that
  state. The closer is reliable at $n=40$ and $n=58$ (both cross-checked) and unreliable at
  $n=345$; its large-$n$ "SOLVED" outputs must not be trusted, and its large-$n$ exhaustion claims
  should be re-derived before use.
* An earlier reading in this session described the descent as "approaching a floor" at 97 conflicts.
  That was wrong twice over: the rate collapse was seed exhaustion, and the state was still 97
  conflicts from a bound.
